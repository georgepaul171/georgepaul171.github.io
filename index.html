Publications and Research Experience

The Adaptive Elastic Horseshoe Prior

Paul, G. and Tipping, M. E. (2026) · under review · manuscript prepared for Electronic Journal of Statistics

The problem. In Bayesian regression, many shrinkage priors commit to a fixed regularisation geometry upfront. Some favour sparsity; others favour grouping correlated predictors. Real datasets often contain both structures at once, and methods that cannot adapt tend to miss one or the other.

The approach. Working closely with Prof. Michael Tipping, I developed a prior that learns this balance from the data during inference, rather than fixing it by hand. The work sits in the broader literature on adaptive regularisation and empirical Bayes: after surveying how existing horseshoe and elastic-net style priors behave under mixed sparse–dense regimes, we proposed a construction that updates its own mixing parameters as evidence accumulates, backed by simulation studies and a real-data application that map when the adaptation helps and when it does not.

Where it goes next. A natural next step is extending the theoretical guarantees to correlated designs — the setting that most motivates the method — and exploring richer likelihoods so that uncertainty estimates remain well calibrated when noise is not constant across observations.

Benchmarking Deep Learning for Engineering Drawing Layout Detection

[Co-authors incl. Paul, G.] (2026) · peer-reviewed, Proceedings of EC3, Corfu · collaborative industry–research project

The problem. Layout-detection models are typically trained and validated on text-heavy documents. Whether those methods transfer to engineering drawings — sparse, line-based, and structurally unlike office paperwork — had not been systematically tested, despite clear practical need in the built-environment sector.

The approach. This was a team effort throughout. Together with domain experts and co-authors, we curated a purpose-built annotated dataset and compared several architectures under matched conditions. My contribution focused on the vision-language arm and the shared evaluation protocol: I worked closely with engineers to define what “correct extraction” meant in practice, then fine-tuned a multimodal model and helped the group run a fair held-out comparison across methods.

Where it goes next. The main finding was a negative one that matters for the field: priors learned on general-document layouts can actively hurt transfer to drawings. A useful future direction is evaluation protocols that treat domain mismatch as a first-class concern — asking not only how well a model scores on a held-out set, but what that score tells us about behaviour under a structurally different deployment distribution.

Hierarchical Bayesian Models for Building Energy Use

MSc research project · 81% · precursor to the Adaptive Elastic Horseshoe Prior work

The problem. Point estimates of building energy use can look precise while hiding how uncertain the prediction really is — a poor fit for carbon assessment, where decisions need transparent uncertainty.

The approach. I investigated hierarchical Bayesian models that pool information across buildings while retaining building-level variation, combining domain-informed priors with flexible likelihoods and using posterior predictive checks to stress-test robustness.

Where it goes next. The project surfaced how much prior choice and likelihood misspecification matter in practice, which directly motivated the adaptive-regularisation questions I later pursued with Prof. Tipping.

Reinforcement Learning with PPO

Academic coursework · collaborative team project

The problem. Teaching an agent effective long-horizon behaviour from sparse rewards is hard: small design choices in representation and reward shape strategy in ways that are easy to get wrong.

The approach. As a team, we trained a PPO agent in a boxing environment, sharing responsibility for architecture, training runs, and analysis. I focused on the feature extractor and on how reward shaping changed long-term play — a useful counterweight to my Bayesian work in thinking about sequential decisions under uncertainty.

Where it goes next. The project reinforced how much evaluation and ablation matter when comparing algorithmic choices, a theme that carries into my applied evaluation work.
