George Paul

Bayesian statistics and machine learning research. Data Science Associate in IBM's AI and Analytics team, London.

My research path runs from an MSc dissertation that grew into a first-author methodological paper on Bayesian shrinkage priors, through an applied research role in industry fine-tuning and rigorously evaluating vision-language and language models — work that became a peer-reviewed EC3 2026 paper. That transition gave me hands-on experience of the gap between how models are evaluated on small held-out sets and how they behave in deployment.

I hold an MSc in Data Science (Distinction) from the University of Bath, where my dissertation was supervised by Prof. Michael Tipping, and a BSc in Mathematics from the University of Manchester.

Outside work

Research

Research is not something I leave at the office. I genuinely enjoy it, and much of my most rewarding work has happened in my own time. The Adaptive Elastic Horseshoe began as my MSc dissertation, and I carried it through to a submitted paper in the evenings and at weekends alongside a full-time job. I'm now doing the same with a follow-up theoretical note on the method.

What draws me in is the combination of rigorous mathematics and practical consequence: a question about how a prior behaves can decide whether a model is trusted in the real world. I spend a good part of my free time reading papers in Bayesian statistics and machine learning, working through derivations, and testing ideas in code to see whether they hold up. I'm always happy to talk with others working on related problems, so please get in touch.

Football

I have played football competitively for most of my life, in China, across Europe and in Africa. Playing in so many different places has shown me how differently the same game can be understood, and it has taught me a lot about teamwork. The best sides I've played in were the ones where cooperation mattered more than individual ability. It is still the main thing that gets me out of my own head.

Running and the gym

I run regularly and train in the gym. It's the routine that keeps the rest of my week balanced, and long runs are often where problems I've been stuck on finally untangle themselves.

Travel

I love travelling, especially to places with striking natural landscapes and cultural diversity. Seeing how different places live and work has broadened how I think about sustainability and the problems that data can help solve.

Formula 1

I've followed Formula 1 for as long as I can remember. It sits right at the intersection of engineering, performance and strategic decision-making under uncertainty: teams make split-second calls on tyres and pit stops with incomplete information, which is a very real-world version of the problems I enjoy working on.
