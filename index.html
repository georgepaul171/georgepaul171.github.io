Research

The Adaptive Elastic Horseshoe Prior

First-author paper with Prof. Michael Tipping · University of Bath

The problem. Shrinkage priors in Bayesian regression fix their regularisation geometry in advance: the horseshoe handles sparse signals well but struggles when predictors are correlated, while elastic-net approaches fix the balance between sparsity and grouping by hand. Neither adapts to data that contains both structures at once.

The approach. I developed a prior that learns that balance from the data itself, updating its mixing and strength parameters during inference via momentum-driven empirical Bayes. I showed these updates correspond to approximate M-steps of an EM algorithm on a variational lower bound, and established minimax-optimal posterior contraction in the sparse normal-means setting. Eight simulation studies map out precisely when the method helps and when it does not, alongside a real-data application.

Where it goes next. The main open problem is extending the contraction theory to correlated designs — precisely the setting that motivates the method. I am also interested in heteroscedastic likelihood specifications to address the calibration limitations the experiments surfaced.

Benchmarking Deep Learning for Engineering Drawing Layout Detection

Peer-reviewed, EC3 2026 · contribution: vision-language model methodology & evaluation

The problem. Document layout models are overwhelmingly trained and validated on text-centric documents. Whether they transfer to engineering drawings — sparse, line-based, extreme aspect ratios — had never been systematically tested.

The approach. Working with domain experts, the team curated a purpose-built annotated dataset and benchmarked five architectures under matched conditions. I delivered the vision-language arm, fine-tuning a multimodal model with parameter-efficient adaptation, and designed the held-out evaluation protocol used across the comparison.

What we found. The multimodal model transferred poorly, and an ablation showed that architectures and weights pretrained on general-document layouts actively underperformed models without that prior — a domain-interference effect. The negative result is the interesting one: representations learned on one distribution can harm transfer to a structurally different one, which bears directly on what evaluation in one setting can tell us about behaviour in another.
