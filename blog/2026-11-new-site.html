The AEH prior and next steps

The Adaptive Elastic Horseshoe prior, my paper with Prof. Michael Tipping, has now been submitted to Communications in Statistics. It started as my MSc dissertation at Bath and I kept developing it alongside full-time work, so seeing it submitted feels like the end of a long first chapter. The idea is a single shrinkage prior that learns, from the data, how to balance sparse and grouped shrinkage, rather than fixing that balance in advance. That matters whenever a few strong signals sit alongside many weak, correlated ones, which is exactly what I first saw in building energy data.

The paper is deliberately clear about what it does not yet establish, and that is where the next piece of work comes in. I'm now building a follow-up theoretical note on the method, focused on two open questions. The first is to derive the adaptive updates from a single objective, so that the procedure for fitting the prior rests on one principled criterion rather than a set of separate update rules. The second is posterior contraction: understanding how quickly, and under what conditions, the posterior concentrates around the truth as more data arrives.

Answering those would put the method on a much firmer theoretical footing, and I'll share how it's going in next month's update.
